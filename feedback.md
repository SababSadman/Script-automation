## 2026-05-24 — Structure — Core Script Formula

**Feedback:** Every script must follow Pain Point → Pain Expansion → Solution → CTA. Never start by saying what the company sells. Start with what the customer is struggling with.

**Underlying Rule:** The script's opening move is always the customer's problem, not the brand's product. The product only appears after the viewer has felt the pain.

**Type:** Correction

**Apply to:** Hook selection, body structure, Step 1 (pain point framing), all modes

## 2026-05-24 — Hook — Negative Hooks Outperform Positive Openers

**Feedback:** Negative hooks should be used most of the time because they stop the scroll faster. They create curiosity, tension, and urgency. However, the whole script should not stay negative — the hook captures attention, the body moves toward opportunity and solution.

**Underlying Rule:** Default to a negative or tension-driven hook. Transition into positive framing by the middle of the body. Never end the script on a negative note.

**Type:** Correction

**Apply to:** Step 2 (visual hook), Step 3 (voice hook), hook selection across all modes

## 2026-05-24 — Hook — Weak Hooks vs. Stronger Negative Hooks

**Feedback:**
- Weak: "Custom merch helps your brand stand out." → Stronger: "Your merch is killing your brand."
- Weak: "Golf apparel can help your team look better." → Stronger: "Your golf merch is hurting your brand reputation."
- Weak: "Branded apparel is useful for events." → Stronger: "Forgettable merch is making your event brand invisible."

**Underlying Rule:** A hook must name a specific cost, risk, or failure — not a generic benefit. The viewer must feel that something is being lost before the solution is introduced.

**Type:** Correction

**Apply to:** Voice hook selection, Step 3, all script modes

## 2026-05-24 — Tone — Scripts Must Be Easy to Say Out Loud

**Feedback:** Every line must be easy to say naturally on camera. If a word feels heavy, formal, or awkward, replace it with something simpler and sharper.

Word replacements:
- Destroys → Kills
- Negatively impacts → Hurts
- Networking opportunities → Brand presence
- Unprofessional appearance → Cheap look
- Fails to create recognition → Gets ignored
- During Stampede event → This Stampede

**Underlying Rule:** Read every line out loud before finalising. If it does not roll off the tongue, rewrite it. Short, sharp, conversational words always beat formal or corporate phrasing.

**Type:** Correction

**Apply to:** Step 7 (Humanize), body writing, hook writing, all modes

## 2026-05-24 — Tone — Speak Directly to the Customer

**Feedback:** Avoid writing about "brands," "companies," or "businesses" in the third person when the message can speak directly to the viewer.
- Weak: "Brands should stand out." → Stronger: "Your brand should stand out."
- Weak: "Businesses need better merch." → Stronger: "Your business needs better merch."

**Underlying Rule:** Always use "your brand / your team / your business / your company / your event / your merch." The customer is the hero of the script, not the brand.

**Type:** Correction

**Apply to:** Body writing, hook selection, Step 4, Step 6, all modes

## 2026-05-24 — Body — Use Bigger, More Attractive Framing for Numbers

**Feedback:** When a number or statistic is used, frame it in a way that feels bigger and more attention-grabbing.
- Less powerful: "Stampede is a 500 million dollar business moment."
- Stronger: "Stampede is a half-a-billion-dollar business moment."

**Underlying Rule:** Always choose the number framing that feels most powerful while remaining accurate. "Half-a-billion" hits harder than "500 million."

**Type:** Correction

**Apply to:** Body writing, pain expansion section, Step 4

---

## 2026-05-24 — Body — Shift to Positive Opportunity After the Hook

**Feedback:** After the negative hook, the body should not insult the viewer or dwell on what they are doing wrong. Shift into opportunity framing: "This is a major chance for your brand to stand out."

**Underlying Rule:** Hook = negative tension. Body = positive opportunity. Never let the script stay critical beyond the opening — frame the solution as an upgrade, not a correction.

**Type:** Correction

**Apply to:** Body writing, Step 4, pain-to-solution transition

---

## 2026-05-24 — Body — Avoid Generic Contrast Phrases

**Feedback:** Phrases like "stand out or blend in" are overused and split attention. When the positive statement is strong enough, use it alone.
- Avoid: "It is an opportunity to either stand out or blend in."
- Use: "It is an exceptional opportunity for your brand to stand out."

**Underlying Rule:** Do not rely on A-vs-B contrast phrasing when the positive outcome is already compelling on its own. Strong single statements beat weak binary choices.

**Type:** Correction

**Apply to:** Body writing, Step 4

## 2026-05-24 — Tone — Keep Scripts Human, Not Corporate

**Feedback:** A strong script should sound like something a real person would say on camera — not a business report or formal presentation.
- Avoid: "Custom apparel provides businesses with enhanced brand visibility during major community events."
- Use: "Custom merch helps your team get noticed when everyone is fighting for attention."

**Underlying Rule:** Write like a smart person explaining the idea clearly, not like a corporate brochure. If it sounds like it was written for a board presentation, rewrite it.

**Type:** Correction

**Apply to:** Step 7 (Humanize), body writing, all modes

## 2026-05-24 — CTA — Must Be Clear and Conversion-Focused

**Feedback:** The CTA must tell the viewer exactly what to do next. No vague asks.
- Weak: "Contact us for more information."
- Stronger: "Submit a free quote and get your Stampede merch started."

Proven CTA formats:
- "Submit a free quote today and get your custom merch started."
- "Request a quote and let us help your team stand out this Stampede."
- "Send us your logo and we will help you build the right merch package."
- "Get your custom apparel started before the event rush begins."

**Underlying Rule:** Every CTA must contain a specific action verb and a specific next step. Push toward a real business action — quote requests, logo submissions, calls, bookings, or inquiries. Never end with "contact us," "reach out," or "learn more."

**Type:** Correction

**Apply to:** Step 5 (CTA), all modes
