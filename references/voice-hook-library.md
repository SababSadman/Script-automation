## How to Use This File

A voice hook is the first thing the presenter or voiceover SAYS — typically in the first 1–3 seconds. It must immediately create curiosity, tension, or a reason to keep watching. Claude selects from this library based on the topic, pain point, and emotional angle of the script.

## Voice Hook Library

### The Redemption Hook ("I Tried Everything")

**Hook Line(s):**
"I tried everything — [option A], [option B], [option C]. Nothing worked. Until I found this."
"We've talked to businesses who tried [alternative], spent thousands, and still ended up with [bad outcome]. Here's what actually works."

**Hook Type:**
Pain-point / Redemption

**Best used for:**
Problem-aware audiences who have already attempted to solve the issue. Positions the brand as the final answer after a series of failed attempts. Highest-converting hook type for warm audiences.

**Notes:**
The "failed attempts" must be specific and real — pull them from actual customer language (reviews, DMs, support tickets). Generic versions ("I tried a lot of things") lose credibility immediately. Source: 2025–2026 Creative Playbook Research Report.

---
### The Swap Hook ("I Swapped This for This")

**Hook Line(s):**
"I swapped [familiar, inferior thing] for [your product] — and here's what happened."
"We used to [old way]. Now we [new way]. The difference is actually shocking."

**Hook Type:**
Contrast / Aspiration

**Best used for:**
TOFU Ad Creative Content. Works when there is a clear, relatable "before" that the audience recognizes. Strong for audiences still using an inferior alternative.

**Notes:**
Top-of-funnel powerhouse. The "familiar thing" being swapped must be something the audience actually uses or considers — not a strawman. Source: 2025–2026 Creative Playbook Research Report.

---
### The Contrarian Hook

**Hook Line(s):**
"[Common belief everyone holds] is not actually why [desired outcome] happens."
"Every [type of business] thinks [common solution] is the answer. It's not."
"Stop doing [common thing]. It's the reason [bad result] keeps happening."

**Hook Type:**
Counterintuitive / Authority

**Best used for:**
Educational content, myth-busting, and authority-building scripts. Works when the brand has a perspective that contradicts what the audience currently believes.

**Notes:**
Creates instant cognitive dissonance — the viewer has to watch to resolve the conflict. The contrarian claim must be defensible and backed up in the body. Source: 2025–2026 Creative Playbook Research Report.

---
### The Scam Hook

**Hook Line(s):**
"Honestly? When I first heard about [brand/offer], I assumed it was a scam."
"[No minimums. Same-day service. Free reprint guarantee.] — Yeah, I thought the same thing. Too good to be true."
"I was skeptical. Then I saw it for myself."

**Hook Type:**
Fear / Curiosity / Trust-Building

**Best used for:**
Cold audience Ad Creative Content where the offer sounds too good to be true. Triggers loss aversion — the viewer watches to resolve whether they are being deceived.

**Notes:**
This hook works because it mirrors the viewer's own skepticism back at them. The resolution in the body must be credible — show proof, show the guarantee, show the result. Source: 2025–2026 Creative Playbook Research Report (Dara Denney).

### The Hands Up / Self-Qualification Hook

**Hook Line(s):**
"Raise your hand if you've ever [relatable frustration]." [pause] "Yeah. Same."
"Who else has dealt with [specific pain point]?"
"If you run a business in Calgary and you've ever had a print order come back completely wrong — this is for you."

**Hook Type:**
Identity / Engagement / Direct Question

**Best used for:**
Organic Social Content and TOFU Ad Creative. Prompts the viewer to mentally opt-in before the pitch begins. The act of self-identifying increases personal investment in the rest of the video.

**Notes:**
The demographic or pain-point call-out must be hyper-specific. "Who wants great results?" is too broad. "Who's ordered custom gear for their team and been disappointed when the colours came out wrong?" is specific enough to stop the right viewer. Source: 2025–2026 Creative Playbook Research Report.

---
### The Listicle Hook

**Hook Line(s):**
"[Number] reasons why [common thing] isn't working for [audience]."
"[Number] things I wish I knew before [doing relevant thing]."
"Here are [number] signs you're working with the wrong print shop."

**Hook Type:**
Curiosity / Value Promise

**Best used for:**
Educational Social Content. Promises digestible, structured value — the viewer knows exactly what they're getting and how long it will take. Strong for retention because the format creates an implicit checklist to complete.

**Notes:**
The number should feel achievable: 3–5 is ideal for short-form. Never promise a list you can't deliver in the video length. Source: 2025–2026 Creative Playbook Research Report.

### The Founder Story Hook

**Hook Line(s):**
"When we started [Brand] in [year], [specific struggle or observation]."
"The reason we built this goes back to one conversation I'll never forget."
"Thirty years ago, [founder name] started this company because [real origin reason]."

**Hook Type:**
Authority / Trust / Aspiration

**Best used for:**
Brand awareness content and warm retargeting scripts. Introduces the "why" behind the brand. Builds trust through personal struggle and redemption. Works especially well for established brands with a real origin story.

**Notes:**
For Budget Promotion: 30 years of history, founded 1994, started as a Calgary-based local operation. The story must be specific and human — avoid corporate speak. Source: 2025–2026 Creative Playbook Research Report.

### The "Give Me Time" Hook

**Hook Line(s):**
"Give me 30 seconds and I'll show you why [result]."
"I just need 15 seconds of your time — this will change how you think about [topic]."
"Stick with me for one minute. What I'm about to show you is worth it."

**Hook Type:**
Curiosity / Commitment

**Best used for:**
Any script where the offer or insight requires a small setup before the payoff. By securing a "time contract" upfront, this hook paradoxically increases hold rate — the viewer has mentally committed to staying.

**Notes:**
The promised timeframe must be accurate. If the video runs longer than stated, trust breaks. Use only when the body genuinely delivers within the promised window. Source: 2025–2026 Creative Playbook Research Report.

### The Identity Call-Out Hook ("If You Are...")

**Hook Line(s):**
"If you're a [specific customer type], this is going to save you a lot of headaches."
"If you run a restaurant, a school, a trades crew — or any business that needs branded gear — listen up."
"If you've ever ordered custom apparel and been let down by what showed up — this is why."

**Hook Type:**
Identity / Local / Direct Question

**Best used for:**
Any content targeting a specific psychographic or customer segment. Immediately tells the right viewer "this is for me" and tells the algorithm exactly who to serve it to. Provides strong creative metadata for Meta's Andromeda targeting.

**Notes:**
The more specific the call-out, the better it performs. "Business owners" is weak. "Calgary business owners who need branded gear for their team before an event" is strong. Source: 2025–2026 Creative Playbook Research Report.

---
### The Price Anchor Hook

**Hook Line(s):**
"This costs less than your daily coffee — and it solves a problem you've had for years."
"For less than [relatable everyday expense], you can have [desirable outcome]."

**Hook Type:**
Offer / Contrast

**Best used for:**
BOFU Ad Creative Content where the viewer is already aware of the brand and the price is a barrier. Anchors cost against a trivial daily expense to reframe perceived value.

**Notes:**
The anchor must be accurate and believable. Do not anchor against something so cheap it insults the product's value. Best used in retargeting campaigns, not cold audiences. Source: 2025–2026 Creative Playbook Research Report.

### The Specificity Hook (Pain Intensifier)

**Hook Line(s):**
"You paid for the order. You waited three weeks. And when the box arrived — the colour was off, the logo was blurry, and you had 100 shirts you couldn't hand out."
"It's not just that it's frustrating. It's that you had an event in three days and no gear."
"Your team showed up in the wrong colours. In front of your biggest client."

**Hook Type:**
Pain-point / Story

**Best used for:**
Any script targeting an audience that has experienced a specific failure or frustration. The "so what" layer — go one level deeper than naming the problem. Show the exact moment it cost them something.

**Notes:**
Replace abstract pain ("you had a bad experience") with the visceral specific moment ("you opened the box the night before the event and the logos were wrong"). Specificity is the only thing that makes a viewer say "that's exactly what happened to me." Source: 2025–2026 Creative Playbook Research Report.

### The Old Way vs. New Way Hook

**Hook Line(s):**
"The old way: [slow, complicated, unreliable process]. The new way: [your brand's streamlined approach]."
"Most businesses still do it this way. [Describe old way.] There's a better option."

**Hook Type:**
Contrast / Counterintuitive

**Best used for:**
Scripts where the brand's process is genuinely faster, simpler, or more reliable than the status quo. Works best when the "old way" is something the audience is actively tolerating.

**Notes:**
Do not fabricate the old way — it must be the real experience of the target audience. For Budget Promotion: "old way" = minimums, slow turnarounds, offshore printing, no accountability. "New way" = in-house, no minimums, same-day, reprint guarantee. Source: 2025–2026 Creative Playbook Research Report.

### The Unique Mechanism Hook

**Hook Line(s):**
"It's not the product. It's [specific mechanism name] — and that's the part no one talks about."
"The reason other print shops can't do this comes down to one thing: [specific capability or process]."
"We have something most print shops don't: 100% in-house production. Here's why that matters."

**Hook Type:**
Authority / Curiosity / Counterintuitive

**Best used for:**
Scripts that need to differentiate the brand from perceived commodity alternatives. Names a specific capability or process ("in-house DTF transfer," "unlimited colours with no minimums") as the reason the brand is different — making the solution feel novel.

**Notes:**
Naming the mechanism creates "on-target curiosity" — it sounds specific enough to be real and superior. Never use vague mechanism names. The mechanism must be something the brand actually has. Source: 2025–2026 Creative Playbook Research Report.

### — Hook Failure Warnings (Reference When Selecting) —

**The Four Horsemen of Failed Voice Hooks — avoid these patterns:**

| Mistake | Description | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Delay | Opening with "What's up guys," greetings, or preamble before the value | Lead with the value or tension in the first 2 seconds |
| Confusion | Viewer can't tell what the video is about within 2 seconds | State the topic or pain point immediately — 5th grade clarity |
| Irrelevance | Viewer does not see their identity in the opening line | Use "you/your" and call out the specific demographic or pain |
| Disinterest | Hook has no contrast, no tension, no curiosity gap | Use A vs. B contrast or open a question that demands an answer |
