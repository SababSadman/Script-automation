### #18 | 16 May 2026 | Short-Form Analytics Framework — Metrics That Matter and When to Pivot

**Category:** What to Avoid / Pacing & Editing

**Description:**
Every metric tells a specific story. Read them together to diagnose what broke and where.

| Metric | What It Tells You |
|---|---|
| Watch time % | Is the content engaging throughout — or dropping off mid-body? |
| Completion rate | Did the hook AND body both deliver? Both must hold. |
| Saves | Is the content valuable enough to revisit? High saves = strong educational or reference value |
| Shares | Is the content worth spreading? High shares = emotional hit or entertainment value |
| Comments | Did the content spark conversation? Question or controversial hooks drive this |
| Follows | Did the viewer want more? Low follows despite high views = content-audience fit problem |

**What to Test (in priority order):**
1. Hooks — same body, different opening. One hook change can 2x results.
2. Length — 15s vs 30s vs 60s for the same concept
3. Format — talking head vs demo vs B-roll heavy
4. CTA — different calls to action on the same content
5. Post time — morning vs afternoon vs evening

**When to Pivot (diagnosis rules):**
- 5+ videos with under 1% completion rate → hooks are failing, change them before anything else
- High views but low follows → CTA is weak or content-audience fit is off
- High saves but low shares → content is valuable but not emotionally charged enough to spread
- High comments but negative → lean into the controversy OR adjust tone if it is damaging trust

**When to Apply:**
After every script round. Use this framework to read analytics from `references/previous-scripts-analytics.md` and diagnose what to fix in the next script — hook, body length, CTA, or format.

**Source:** Short-Form Video Reference Guide (short-form-video.md)

### #17 | 16 May 2026 | Short-Form Video Structures — Story Arc and POV/Skit

**Category:** Ad Structure / Pacing & Editing

**Description:**
Two proven short-form structures beyond the standard hook-body-CTA format.

---

**The Story Arc (45–60 seconds)**
Best for: Personal stories, case studies, testimonials, transformation content.

```
[0–3s]   Hook: Tease the outcome — don't explain it yet
[3–15s]  Setup: Context and stakes — why this matters
[15–45s] Journey: What actually happened
[45–55s] Resolution: The result
[55–60s] Lesson or CTA
```

The key rule: the hook must tease the resolution without giving it away. The viewer stays because they need the ending. Apply the Interest Loop principle — open the gap at 0s, close it at 55s.

---

**The POV/Skit (15–30 seconds)**
Best for: Relatable content, humor, niche communities, social content.

```
[0–3s]   Setup: Text overlay sets the scene ("POV: You just received your branded order...")
[3–25s]  Performance: Act out the relatable scenario
[25–30s] Punchline or twist
```

The key rule: the text overlay at the start does the targeting — it tells the right viewer "this is about you" before a single word is spoken. The punchline must pay off the setup completely.

**When to Apply:**
- Story Arc: Ad Creative Content for MOFU/BOFU audiences who need to understand the brand's track record or process before buying
- POV/Skit: Social Content for organic reach and niche community building — also effective as TOFU ad creative when the scenario is highly relatable to the ICP

**Source:** Short-Form Video Reference Guide (short-form-video.md)

### #16 | 16 May 2026 | Short-Form Hook Templates by Category

**Category:** Hook Pattern

**Description:**
Four hook categories with proven template lines for short-form video. Each category is optimized for a different viewer response — select based on the goal of the content.

---

**Curiosity Hooks** — Best for engagement and watch time
- "The secret to [outcome] that nobody talks about"
- "I found the one thing about [topic] that changes everything"
- "I tried [thing] for 30 days and I was NOT expecting this"
- "Nobody talks about this, but..."
- "The reason [common thing] doesn't work is..."
- "Am I the only one who didn't know this?"

---

**Value Hooks** — Best for saves and educational content
- "How to [achieve outcome] in [specific timeframe]"
- "[Number] things that will [benefit]"
- "Everything you need to know about [topic] in 60 seconds"
- "If you're struggling with [problem], watch this"
- "Stop doing [common practice] — here's why"
- "[Number] mistakes that are killing your [results]"

---

**Story Hooks** — Best for watch time and brand trust
- "3 months ago, I [bad state]. Today, I [good state]."
- "Here's how I went from [before] to [after]"
- "I used to think [old belief]. Then [event] changed everything."
- "I made a huge mistake with [topic]"
- "Here's why I stopped [common practice]"

---

**Controversial Hooks** — Best for comments and reach
- "Unpopular opinion: [bold statement]"
- "[Common advice] is actually wrong"
- "I'm going to get hate for this, but..."
- "Most [audience type] get this completely wrong"

---

**Selection Rule:**
Match the hook category to the goal — not just the topic.
- Want saves? → Value Hook
- Want comments and reach? → Controversial Hook
- Want long watch time? → Story Hook
- Want pure scroll-stop and curiosity? → Curiosity Hook

For Ad Creative Content, Curiosity and Story hooks typically outperform Controversial ones (controversy can spike comments but hurt brand trust). For Social Content, all four are valid.

**When to Apply:**
Step 3 of the AurixScript pipeline — when selecting the voice hook. Cross-reference this template library with the voice hook library entries to find the strongest match for the topic and content goal.

**Source:** Short-Form Video Reference Guide (short-form-video.md)

### #15 | 16 May 2026 | Pattern Interrupt → Pivot (Bait and Switch Hook)

**Category:** Hook Pattern / Ad Structure

**Description:**
Open with a completely unrelated, high-stakes dramatic scenario (survival, disaster, danger) that has zero obvious connection to the brand. Use it to deliver a real, counterintuitive insight that builds authority and tension. Then pivot to the brand through a single thematic thread — a word, concept, or idea that bridges both worlds. Close with a callback line that ties the dramatic scenario back to the brand one final time.

The structure is:
1. **Pattern Interrupt** — Dramatic scenario headline, completely unexpected ("HOW TO SURVIVE FALLING OUT OF A PLANE")
2. **Misconception Debunk** — State what everyone believes, then break it with a real fact. This builds credibility before the viewer knows it's an ad.
3. **The Pivot Line** — One sentence that establishes the thematic bridge ("You're not looking for soft — you're looking for the right landing spot.")
4. **Brand Introduction** — Direct and confident: "For example — [Brand]." No soft entry.
5. **Stakes Bridge** — One line that carries the emotional weight of the hook into the brand context ("When you can't afford to land wrong — they're the spot.")
6. **Features as Punches** — Short, staccato proof points. Not a paragraph — individual hits.
7. **Reframe Line** — A single closing line that redefines the brand through the hook's metaphor ("That's not a print shop. That's a landing strip.")
8. **CTA Callback** — The final line returns to the hook's world one last time ("If you're going to make a leap — land with Budget Promotion.")

**The Bridge Rule:**
The pivot from dramatic scenario to brand must travel through a single word or concept — not a full explanation. The word does the work. In the airplane example: "landing spot" connects falling from a plane to choosing the right vendor. The viewer's brain makes the leap; you just hand them the thread.

**The Bridge Failure Mode:**
Jumping from the pivot line directly into a feature list kills the momentum. The bridge needs one emotional stakes sentence before features land — otherwise it reads as a gear-shift, not a reveal.

**When to Apply:**
Ad Creative Content for cold audiences on Meta/Instagram. Highest impact when the brand's offer is straightforward (custom apparel, food, simple services) but needs to compete in a crowded feed. The mismatch between hook and product IS the entertainment — it earns attention the brand alone could not.

**What Makes the Closing Callback Work:**
The final line must return to the hook's exact language, not a paraphrase. "If you have to fall, fall here" and "If you're going to make a leap — land with Budget Promotion" both work because they echo the hook word-for-word. This creates a satisfying loop closure that viewers remember and share.

**Example (Budget Promotion — Airplane Hook):**
- Hook scenario: How to survive falling out of a plane
- Misconception debunked: Aim for water → wrong, water doesn't compress at 200mph
- Thematic thread: "landing spot"
- Pivot line: "You're not looking for soft — you're looking for the right landing spot. For example — Budget Promotion in Calgary."
- Stakes bridge: "When you can't afford to land wrong — they're the spot."
- Reframe: "That's not a print shop. That's a landing strip."
- CTA callback: "If you're going to make a leap — land with Budget Promotion."

**Source:** AurixLab session — 16 May 2026. Adapted from Houston Texas Hot Chicken viral ad framework.

### #14 | 16 May 2026 | EGC (Employee-Generated Content) Builds Identity-Level Loyalty

**Category:** Ad Structure / Emotional Angle

**Description:**
Using founders or staff on camera (Employee-Generated Content) humanizes the brand and builds "Why"-based loyalty. The viewer doesn't just buy a product — they buy into a person or team they trust. When done right (e.g., a founder calling a customer to say thank you), it moves the product from utility to identity marker. Viewers emulate the type of customer being praised.

**When to Apply:**
Brand awareness and trust-building ads. Works especially well when re-targeting warm audiences or for any content where authenticity and human connection are the primary goal.

**Example:**
Humantra "Founder/Employee Phone Call" — founder calls a customer to thank them. Viewer builds identity by emulating the praised customer archetype.

### #13 | 16 May 2026 | Interest Loops — Open a Curiosity Gap, Close It at the End

**Category:** Hook Pattern / Body Pattern

**Description:**
Open a psychological "curiosity gap" in the hook that is only resolved at the very end of the video. The viewer is compelled to watch through in order to get the resolution. This dramatically increases hold rate and watch completion.

**When to Apply:**
Any video content where retention is the goal. Especially useful for TOFU ads and organic Reels where the viewer has no reason to stay unless something is left unresolved.

**Example:**
Hook: "We almost turned this client away — here's why we're glad we didn't." The answer is only given at the end after the full story plays out.

**Source:** 2026 Creative-Centric Growth Blueprint

### #12 | 16 May 2026 | "Scam Hook" — Visceral Scroll-Stopping Interrupt

**Category:** Hook Pattern

**Description:**
Dara Denney's framework. Open with something like "I thought this was a scam — until I tried it." This is a visceral pattern interrupt that triggers both skepticism (relatable) and curiosity (what changed their mind?). It is one of the most reliable TOFU hooks for cold audiences.

**When to Apply:**
Ad Creative Content targeting cold audiences with zero brand awareness. Works best when the product or service is unconventional, premium-priced, or sounds "too good to be true" on its face.

**Example:**
"Honestly? When I first heard about [Brand], I assumed it was another scam. No minimums, same-day service, price match guarantee — yeah, right. But then..."

### #11 | 16 May 2026 | "If You" Hook — Tribal Identity Targeting

**Category:** Hook Pattern

**Description:**
Open with "If you're [identity descriptor]..." to immediately self-select the target viewer. The viewer hears themselves described and stops scrolling because the content feels made for them specifically. This is how the creative performs targeting without relying on platform interest groups.

**When to Apply:**
Any content targeting a specific psychographic or customer category. Especially powerful for B2B content where the audience is narrow and clearly defined.

**Example:**

### #10 | 16 May 2026 | Hold Rate Warning — Hook Relevance Over Hook Strength

**Category:** What to Avoid

**Description:**
Fraser Cottrell's warning: a high hook-in rate means nothing if the hook isn't directly relevant to the product or offer. Viewers who click in for an irrelevant hook will drop off immediately, causing the hold rate to plummet and producing zero conversions. The hook must capture the right attention, not just any attention.

**When to Apply:**
Every script. Before finalizing the hook, ask: "Would the person most likely to buy from this brand keep watching after this hook?" If not, the hook is wrong — no matter how strong it is.

**Example:**
A hook about "the one thing that ruined my morning" may spike curiosity but drive irrelevant viewers. A hook about "why your company t-shirts never look as good as you imagined" gets the right person to stop.

**Source:** 2026 Creative-Centric Growth Blueprint (Fraser Cottrell)

### #9 | 16 May 2026 | Mini-VSSL Format — 60–90s Video Sales Letter for Social Feeds

**Category:** Ad Structure

**Description:**
A compressed Video Sales Letter format designed for short-form feeds. Runs 60–90 seconds. Heavy on education and story. Positions the product through authority, trust signals, and a clear outcome. Works at MOFU (middle of funnel) where the viewer already has some awareness. Higher production than a typical Reel; feels more credible.

**When to Apply:**
Retargeting campaigns, warm audiences, or any ad where the offer needs explanation. Especially useful for services that seem complex (custom printing, branded merch, B2B offers).

### #8 | 16 May 2026 | Investment Hook Framework — Script from "Failed Attempts"

**Category:** Hook Pattern / Ad Structure

**Description:**
Dara Denney's top-performing trust-building framework. Pull customer reviews and find language about the time, money, or effort spent on failed alternatives before finding the brand. Script the hook around that "investment in the struggle." This mirrors the viewer's exact emotional state and creates instant recognition.

**How to Source the Language:**
Export customer reviews or testimonials. Search specifically for "failed attempts" — phrases like "I tried everything," "wasted so much money on," "nothing worked until." Use this verbatim or near-verbatim in the hook.

**When to Apply:**
TOFU and MOFU Ad Creative Content. Most powerful when the product solves a problem the audience has tried (and failed) to solve before.

**Example:**
"We've talked to hundreds of businesses who spent thousands on print orders that came back wrong, had to fight for refunds, and still ended up without the gear they needed. That's exactly why we built our reprint guarantee."

**Source:** 2026 Creative-Centric Growth Blueprint (Dara Denney)

### #7 | 16 May 2026 | Hero vs. Villain Scripting Framework

**Category:** Ad Structure

**Description:**
Fraser Cottrell's framework. Structure: Hook (curiosity or visual disrupt) → Villain Introduction (the problem, bad habit, or inferior alternative) → Hero Introduction (your product as the savior) → Proof or Demo (a physical test that shows the hero winning) → CTA. The product is never positioned alone — it always wins against something.

**When to Apply:**
Ad Creative Content comparing the brand's offer to the alternative (doing it manually, using a competitor, tolerating the current pain). Works especially well when there is a clear "before" state that is visibly worse.

**Example:**
Villain: "Most print shops have minimums, long lead times, and zero accountability when something goes wrong." Hero: "Budget Promotion — no minimums, same-day service, and if it's not right, we reprint it. Free."

**Source:** 2026 Creative-Centric Growth Blueprint (Fraser Cottrell)

### #6 | 16 May 2026 | PAS AI Framework — Problem → Agitate → Solution with Visual Agitation

**Category:** Ad Structure

**Description:**
The classic PAS framework supercharged with visual agitation. Name the problem. Then visually or verbally agitate it — show what it costs the person (emotionally, financially, in time). Then introduce the solution. The agitation step is where most scripts are weak — it must go deeper than naming the problem, it must make the viewer feel it.

**When to Apply:**
Ad Creative Content at any funnel stage. Most powerful when the pain point is already familiar to the audience — the script just names it more precisely than they could.

**Example:**
Problem: "Your branded merch doesn't look the way you imagined." Agitation: "You ordered 100 shirts, paid upfront, waited three weeks, and when the box arrived — the colour was off, the logo was blurry, and you had 100 pieces you couldn't hand out." Solution: "Budget Promotion — in-house printing, mock-up approval, and a reprint guarantee if it's not exactly right."

**Source:** 2026 Creative-Centric Growth Blueprint

### #5 | 16 May 2026 | 10-Hook Rule — Volume-Led Iteration

**Category:** Ad Structure / Pacing & Editing

**Description:**
One hook variation can 2x results for the same body video. Sabri Suby and Fraser Cottrell both validate this. For every high-performing video body, shoot or write at least 7–10 distinct hook variations. Test all of them. You are always "one hook away" from a breakthrough — the body and offer may be strong, but the hook is the gatekeeper.

**When to Apply:**
Every Ad Creative Content campaign. Never launch a new concept without at least 7 hook variants ready to test. For organic Social Content, rotate hooks seasonally.

**Example:**
Same body video about Budget Promotion's reprint guarantee gets 10 hooks: "If you're a business owner…", "I thought this was a scam…", "The reason your branded merch always disappoints you…", "This Calgary print shop has a guarantee no one else offers…", etc.

**Source:** 2026 Creative-Centric Growth Blueprint (Sabri Suby, Fraser Cottrell)

### #4 | 16 May 2026 | 5th Grade Reading Level — Clarity Scales, Complexity Fails

**Category:** Body Pattern / Pacing & Editing

**Description:**
Chase Chappell notes that the highest-performing creators (Mr. Beast, Steve Jobs) use 5th-grade vocabulary. Ultimate clarity bypasses the "confusion filter" that triggers scrolling. If the viewer has to think to understand, they scroll. Use LLMs to rewrite all scripts to a 5th-grade reading level before finalizing.

**When to Apply:**
Every script — Ad Creative and Social Content both. Run all final scripts through a readability check. Remove jargon, long sentences, and multi-clause structures.

**Example:**
Instead of: "Our proprietary decoration process leverages advanced DTF transfer technology to deliver superior chromatic fidelity." → "We print your logo in sharp colour — on anything — and it lasts."

**Source:** 2026 Creative-Centric Growth Blueprint (Chase Chappell)

### #3 | 16 May 2026 | Visual Targeting — Relatability as Audience Signaling

**Category:** Hook Pattern / Ad Structure

**Description:**
In the Meta Andromeda era, the creative performs the targeting. Visuals must signal the intended audience to the algorithm and to the viewer simultaneously. If targeting business owners, shoot in an office or at a branded event — not in a studio. The viewer self-identifies with the environment before they even hear the copy. Background, wardrobe, and setting are not aesthetic choices — they are targeting choices.

**When to Apply:**
Every video script. When writing the Visual Hook section, always specify a setting that matches the target customer's daily reality — not what looks good in isolation.

**Example:**
Targeting restaurant owners → film in a real commercial kitchen or dining room, not a neutral studio. Targeting trades crews → film at a job site or in a warehouse, not against a white wall.

**Source:** 2026 Creative-Centric Growth Blueprint (Ben Trogrlic)

### #2 | 16 May 2026 | Soft CTA for Cold Audiences, Hard CTA for Warm Audiences

**Category:** CTA Pattern

**Description:**
On cold audiences with no prior brand awareness, soft CTAs ("Save this", "Follow for more", "DM us if this sounds familiar") consistently outperform direct response CTAs ("Buy now", "Book today"). Hard CTAs work best once the viewer has been warmed through prior content or retargeting.

**When to Apply:**
Organic Social Content targeting new audiences → soft CTA. Ad Creative Content for warm/retargeted audiences → hard CTA. For TOFU paid ads, consider a mid-weight CTA: "Get a free quote" is more accessible than "Order now."

**Example:**
Cold reel: "Save this if you've ever had a print order come back wrong." Warm retargeting ad: "Book your free quote at budgetpromotion.ca — takes 60 seconds."

**Source:** 2026 Creative-Centric Growth Blueprint

### #1 | 16 May 2026 | Creative is the Targeting — Meta Andromeda Era Principle

**Category:** Ad Structure / Strategic Framework

**Description:**
In 2026, Meta's algorithm (referred to as "Andromeda") no longer targets based on manual interest groups. It targets based on which creatives deserve to be shown — determined by the creative's ability to retain attention. 80% of ad performance is front-loaded in research and creative development. The ad is the focus group. Creative assets have superseded technical targeting as the primary driver of scale. Brands must shift from clinical selling to persona-based storytelling built on deep "bruised knee" research — finding the exact visceral frustrations of the prospect.

**When to Apply:**
This is a foundational principle, not a single tactic. It should inform every script: the research that precedes it, the hook that opens it, the language used in the body, and the specificity of the CTA. Never write a generic script and expect targeting to do the work.

**Source:** 2026 Creative-Centric Growth Blueprint (Ben Trogrlic, Sabri Suby, Dara Denney, Fraser Cottrell, Chase Chappell)

## Reel Script Output Format

Every reel script must follow the structure below. The script should be written in a clean, production-friendly format so the speaker, videographer, and editor can understand it easily.

---

### Required Structure

```
Topic - [Reel topic]

Voice Hook

Opal bhai: "[Opening hook line]"
Shot 01: [Clear description of the opening shot, camera framing, action, prop usage, and visual direction.]

Body

Opal bhai: "[First body line]"
Shot 01: [Shot direction for this line.]

Opal bhai: "[Second body line]"
Shot 01: [Shot direction for this line.]

Opal bhai: "[Third body line]"
Shot 01: [Shot direction for this line.]

CTA

Opal bhai: "[Final call-to-action line]"
Shot 01: [Final shot direction, preferably direct-to-camera or action-based.]
```

---

### Formatting Rules

- Always start with `Topic - [Topic Name]`
- Use `Voice Hook`, `Body`, and `CTA` as the three main section labels — no other section names
- Every spoken line must begin with `Opal bhai:`
- Every spoken line must be immediately followed by `Shot 01:` with a clear visual direction on the next line
- Shot directions must specify: camera framing, action, props, tone, and any editing idea when relevant
- Keep the script easy to shoot, easy to read, and easy to edit
- Do not add explanatory text before the script — go straight to `Topic -`
- Anything added after the CTA — editor notes, production notes, reminders, optional ideas — must be placed under a separate markdown heading using `#`

---

### Reference Example

```
Topic - Get Stampede-ready now!

Voice Hook

Opal bhai: "Stampede is just around the corner and here's the mistake that costs brands REAL exposure."
Shot 01: Talking-head shot of Opal bhai holding an iPad in one hand and pointing at it with the other hand. Blur the iPad display to make the audience curious about what the iPad is hiding.

Body

Opal bhai: "When Calgary Stampede hits, everybody wants hats, western shirts, team gear, and branded pieces at the same time."
Shot 01: Medium-wide talking-head shot with a display table beside him showing hats, denim, and western-style apparel. He gestures toward the products while naming them.

Opal bhai: "Rush doesn't always translate well to quality. So, brands that don't prepare early get left behind."
Shot 01: Medium close-up of Opal bhai holding one premium product in hand while speaking in a calm expert tone.

Opal bhai: "It's a 640 million dollar business opportunity and being left behind will hurt! So, choose the right western look, before the rush."
Shot 01: Front-facing shot of Opal bhai holding two different apparel options and visually comparing them while he speaks.

CTA

Opal bhai: "So if your business wants to look ready for Stampede, send us your quote now and let our team help you build it before the rush."
Shot 01: Strong direct-to-camera talking-head shot with Opal bhai standing near a desk or monitor where the quote page is visible in the background.
```
